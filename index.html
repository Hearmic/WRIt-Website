Why it happens

Ontario's rent cap has two holes.

Ontario does cap rent increases. For 2026 the cap is 2.1% [6]. But it only protects you while you stay put, and it doesn't cover newer buildings at all.

01 · VACANCY DECONTROL

When a tenant moves out, the landlord can set the next tenant's rent at any amount. The cap starts over from the new, higher number.

02 · THE 2018 EXEMPTION

Units first occupied after November 15, 2018 aren't covered by the guideline at all [7]. Much of the new supply has no cap.

03 · THE RESULT

Landlords earn more when a unit turns over, so tenants stay in places that no longer fit them because moving costs thousands a year.

"But aren't rents falling?"

Asking rents are down. That doesn't close the gap.

It's true: advertised rents in Toronto have dropped from their … peak [2][1]. More supply is a good thing. But even after that drop, the average asking rent for a 2-bedroom is still about $… a month above what Toronto tenants actually pay on average [1][4]. The decline helps people who are about to move a little. It does nothing for the rule that resets your rent every time you do.

What changed on September 21, 2026

Falling behind on rent now gives you half the time to catch up.

Under Bill 60, once a landlord serves an N4 notice for unpaid rent, the tenant has 7 days to pay it off and keep the tenancy, down from 14 [8]. Every tenant who can't catch up in time loses a unit that then resets to market rent for the next person.

Who it hits

Young people move more often, so they pay the reset more often.

A first apartment, moving in with a partner, needing a room for a kid: every one of those means signing a new lease at the new-tenant price. And more young people are renters than a decade ago.

It's been done

New York closed this hole in 2019.

New York's Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act ended vacancy decontrol for its regulated apartments, so rent no longer jumps just because a tenant leaves [9]. Ontario had a version of this protection before the late 1990s too, so this would be restoring something the province already had.

What we're asking for

Stop the reset.

  1. A public rent registry

    Landlords report the rent for each unit, so a new tenant can see what the last tenant paid. It's cheap, and it's needed before any other rule can be enforced.

  2. Vacancy control

    Cap how much rent can go up between one tenant and the next, for example the annual guideline plus a fixed margin, instead of letting it reset to any amount.

  3. Keep building

    Rent rules can discourage new construction, so new buildings would keep their exemption for a fixed number of years before the cap applies. That way developers can still recover their costs.
